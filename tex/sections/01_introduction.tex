\section{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}

When a small language model (SLM) is asked a factual question about
something it never learned, it either refuses or answers anyway. A
fraction of those answers happen to be correct; the rest are
confabulations: fluent, confident, and wrong.
The term, argued for over ``hallucination'' by
\citet{smith2023confabulation} and adopted in the machine-learning
literature by \citet{farquhar2024semantic}, names pattern-based
reconstruction the
model does not recognize as wrong. Can we read that choice off the
hidden state, and steer it? \citet{kadavath2022know} argued that LLMs
``mostly know what they know.'' \citet{alain2017probes} introduced
diagnostic linear classifiers, or \emph{probes}, to evaluate what a hidden
state encodes. A correctness-probing literature followed
\citep{azaria2023internal, marks2024geometry, orgad2024show, kossen2024semantic}, fitting probes to predict
whether an answer will be right and reporting accuracies in the \(80\)--\(90\%\) range. A
high probe accuracy is not by itself evidence that the hidden state
knows anything. In adjacent settings, recent work traces above-chance
accuracy to the prompt rather than the model's internal state:
introspection \citep{singh2026introspect} and reasoning mode
\citep{sahoo2026linear}. A confabulation probe faces the same question.
Does it read the model's state, or the question?

Controls that separate hidden-state signal from what the prompt alone
predicts remain rare in the correctness-probing literature, where
time-sensitive question sets routinely conflate ``post-cutoff'' with
``model doesn't know.'' Refusal steering by a single direction has been shown for safety refusal \citep{arditi2024refusal} and for entity knowledge, via entity-recognition latents in Gemma-2 SAEs \citep{ferrando2025entity}. \citet{wollschlager2025geometry} subsequently showed that safety refusal is mediated by a cone of directions rather than a single one, several of them representationally independent and each sufficient for steering; attribution-graph analysis of Claude 3.5 Haiku \citep{lindsey2025biology} locates hallucination in a default ``can't answer'' pathway that known-entity features suppress, the same organization \citeauthor{ferrando2025entity} report for Gemma 2. I ask whether a single direction likewise converts most confabulations into refusals: epistemic abstention on facts the model never learned.

I map the geometry of this decision with these probes, a sparse
autoencoder, and direct optimization of intervention directions, and
test whether steering along the recovered directions turns
confabulations into refusals, under controls that isolate the hidden
state's own contribution. I introduce \textbf{ConfabQA}, a probing benchmark of 4 domains
\(\times\) 3 categories (well-known pre-cutoff, \emph{obscure}
pre-cutoff, post-cutoff); the third category, items whose answers were
in the training data but which the model is expected to fail, populates
the cell standard pre-vs-post-cutoff designs leave empty, breaking the
cutoff/correctness confound. ConfabQA, plus 800-item samples of PopQA
\citep{mallen2023popqa} and TriviaQA \citep{joshi2017triviaqa}, run through three
instruction-tuned models in the \(1.7\)--\(3\)B range (Qwen3-1.7B,
Gemma 2 2B, Llama 3.2 3B) with Qwen3-4B as a within-family scaling
control; every probe is scored against both the majority baseline and a
stricter question-text baseline, the control that decides whether the
hidden state contributed anything.

\textbf{Findings.} Two complementary results emerge:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  \emph{Refusal vs.~confabulation is a model-internal feature: each
  model linearly encodes its own upcoming speech act.} On the
  wrong+refusal subset of ConfabQA the late-layer probe beats the
  strongest prompt baseline on every model: \(+7.4\) pp on Qwen3-1.7B,
  \(+16.3\) pp on Gemma 2 2B, \(+2.2\) pp on Llama 3.2 3B (the Llama margin
  is within fold-to-fold spread; its powered counterpart is the
  Section 8.2 refusal-channel gap). That some such signal exists is
  natural (the label is the model's own output decision); the findings
  are its \emph{form}: a single linear direction in the late layers
  (Gemma peaks mid-network, at 19 of 26), causally sufficient for the
  first-token refusal opening, and decomposable. A one-shot hidden-state
  intervention drives the first-token refusal-opener rate to \(100\%\)
  on Qwen3 and Gemma (Sections 6.2 and 7.2), though on Qwen3 only about a third of these openings complete as judged refusals, the rest reverting to content (Gemma completes \(87\%\) at a super-norm dose and \(30\)--\(40\%\) at sub-norm); closing that gap is left to future work. This replicates \citeauthor{arditi2024refusal}'s (\citeyear{arditi2024refusal}) single-direction finding in the
  epistemic-abstention rather than safety-refusal regime, with the cutoff
  variable controlled by construction (mean-difference steering lineage:
  \citealp{panickssery2024caa}; answerability readable from pre-generation
  states: \citealp{slobodkin2023curious}; known-unknowns benchmarks:
  \citealp{yin2023know}). The contribution is the cutoff control, the causal
  completion, and the decomposition. A sparse-autoencoder resolves the
  direction into a deferential-register ensemble (canonical refusal-opener
  plus apology, officialese, and hedging registers; Section 6.3), of which
  the opener feature alone flips \(30/30\) wrong-item next-token argmaxes
  to refusal openers. The reverse search finds no counterpart: no
  concentrated dictionary feature or token signature picks out
  confabulation, which behaves as the default attempt pathway missing a
  fact (Section 6.3).
\item
  \emph{Llama 3.2 3B carries a substantially larger and more general
  correctness signal than Qwen3-1.7B or Gemma 2 2B.} On balanced PopQA /
  TriviaQA subsamples, Llama's probe beats the prompt baseline by
  \(+21\)--\(+25\) pp, \(5.7\times\) and \(2.2\times\) Qwen's margins on
  the same benchmarks. Three controls localize the source: not parameter
  count (the Qwen3-4B scaling control moves the margin by less than
  \(2\) pp; Section 8.1); not dataset-specific overfit (Llama keeps
  \(\approx 81\%\) of its margin under cross-dataset transfer without
  refit, where Qwen's and Gemma's transfer collapses; Section 8.3); not
  refusal-channel readout (\(\approx 83\%\) survives dropping refusals,
  and an independent refusal probe adds a further \(+18\)--\(+25\) pp
  gap; Section 8.2). Gemma is behaviorally intermediate, yet its
  correctness probe is the most dataset-local of the three; the most
  plausible single explanation is Llama's heavy calibrated-abstention
  training forcing a content-invariant ``model is uncertain''
  representation that neither Qwen3's nor Gemma's training developed.
\end{enumerate}
