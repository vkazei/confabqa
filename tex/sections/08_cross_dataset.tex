\section{8. Cross-dataset results}\label{cross-dataset-results}

Do they survive a change of question distribution? This section moves
from ConfabQA to PopQA and TriviaQA: bootstrap confidence intervals with
a within-family scaling control, a refusal-channel attribution, and a
no-refit transfer test.

% ----- 8.1 Balanced-subsample bootstrap (ConfabQA, PopQA, TriviaQA) -----
\subsection{8.1 Balanced-subsample bootstrap (ConfabQA, PopQA,
TriviaQA)}\label{balanced-subsample-bootstrap-confabqa-popqa-triviaqa}

Are the single-seed \(h_{adds}\) point estimates robust to sampling
noise, and do the conclusions transfer to broader factual benchmarks?
The Section 4 bootstrap protocol runs on \textbf{nine} ConfabQA cells
plus \textbf{five} external-dataset cells (PopQA \(\times\)
\{Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, Llama 3.2 3B\} + TriviaQA \(\times\)
\{Qwen3-1.7B, Llama 3.2 3B\}), fourteen
\texttt{(dataset,\ model,\ target)} combinations (label provenance in
Appendix E); the Qwen3-4B PopQA cell is the within-family scaling
control isolating parameter count from family / post-training recipe.

\textbf{Reading the fourteen cells (Table \ref{tbl:bootstrap} and
forest plot, Appendix E).} \(5\) of the \(9\) ConfabQA cells, and
\(5/5\) external-dataset cells, have 95\% CIs excluding \(0\); the Qwen3
and Gemma disconfounded within-stratum cells remain consistent with zero
but with positive point estimates in all four cases, so the single-seed
``null'' reading was borderline rather than emphatic. The CIs are
per-cell and unadjusted for multiple comparisons; the cross-model
contrast rests on no single borderline cell. Two protocol checks
calibrate them (Appendix E): a label-permutation null shows
\(\approx 1\) pp-range margins should not be read as evidence while the
all-items margins sit far above the null, and an item-level bootstrap
leaves both Llama within-stratum cells consistent with zero, so those
two positive CIs should be read as point estimates.

\textbf{Cross-dataset \(\times\) cross-model contrast.} On the same
PopQA data under the same substring-correct criterion (Qwen pooled over
three seeds, Llama single-seed; Section 4), Llama recovers a
\(+24.9\) pp correctness margin where Qwen3-1.7B recovers \(+4.4\) pp, a
\(5.7\times\) gap on \(1.8\times\) the parameter count; on TriviaQA the
gap is \(2.2\times\), and it is driven neither by raw accuracy nor by
the prompt-baseline floor (accounting in Appendix E): Llama's hidden
state is qualitatively richer in linearly-decodable correctness
information.

\textbf{Within-family scaling control (Qwen3-4B).} Qwen3-4B (same
family, \(2.4\times\) Qwen3-1.7B's parameter count, \(\approx 25\%\)
more than Llama 3.2 3B) lands at \(+5.77\) pp, 95\% CI
\([+2.34, +11.61]\): statistically indistinguishable from Qwen3-1.7B's
\(+4.35\) pp and far below Llama's \(+24.94\) pp. Doubling parameter count
within the Qwen family moves \(\bar h_{adds}\) by \(\approx 1.4\) pp;
switching family at smaller parameter count moves it by
\(\approx 19\) pp: \textbf{the cross-model gap is not a parameter-count
effect}; it tracks model family / post-training recipe (corroborating
raw accuracies in Appendix E; artifact list in Appendix B.4).

% ----- 8.2 Refusal-channel attribution -----
\subsection{8.2 Refusal-channel
attribution}\label{refusal-channel-attribution}

The up-to-\(5.7\times\) \(h_{adds}\) gap invites a mechanical
explanation: the probe target labels ``wrong'' together with
``refusal,'' and Llama refuses far more than Qwen on these benchmarks
(PopQA \(62.9\%\) vs.~\(1.7\%\); census in Appendix E), so its
\(h_{adds}\) could be refusal-channel readout rather than factual
self-knowledge: the ``refusal direction is the whole atlas'' hypothesis,
extended to external data.

Two tests run under the same bootstrap protocol on the four cells with
appreciable refusal counts: \emph{Test A} (drop refusals) filters the
pool to \texttt{judge\_label} \(\in\) \{\texttt{correct},
\texttt{wrong}\}, balances 50/50, and re-probes, so any surviving
\(h_{adds}\) is genuine correct-vs-wrong discrimination among attempted
answers; \emph{Test B} (probe refusal directly) targets
\texttt{judge\_label\ ==\ \textquotesingle{}refusal\textquotesingle{}}
balanced 50/50, measuring how cleanly the abstention decision is encoded
beyond prompt-text predictability.

\textbf{Headline.} Dropping refusals leaves Llama's margin largely
intact (\(+24.94 \to +20.76\) on PopQA, \(+21.25 \to +17.71\) on
TriviaQA): the refusal channel accounts for \(\approx 17\%\) of the
headline and the bulk (\(\approx 83\%\)) is real correct-vs-wrong
discrimination on attempted items, while the Qwen rows are essentially
unchanged (Qwen refuses too rarely here for the confound to bite).
Probed directly, Llama's refusal target beats the strongest prompt
baseline by \(+18.44\) (PopQA) and \(+25.43\) pp (TriviaQA) with CIs
excluding \(0\) by wide margins; Qwen's analogous margins are positive
but under-powered. One caveat: the ratio's numerator is judge-labeled
and its denominator substring-labeled, and a label-sensitivity rerun
shows the contrast is not a label artifact (full table, per-cell
readings, and the rerun in Appendix E, Table \ref{tbl:refchannel}).
Llama 3.2 3B thus carries two independent linearly-decodable signals,
correct-vs-wrong among attempted answers (Test A) and a clean abstention
direction (Test B), with correctness the larger share: the ``refusal
direction is the whole atlas'' framing was right about Qwen3 and wrong
about Llama.

% ----- 8.3 Transfer of the correctness probe -----
\subsection{8.3 Transfer of the correctness
probe}\label{transfer-of-the-correctness-probe}

Is the probe direction itself portable: does a correctness probe fit on
dataset \(A\) classify dataset \(B\) without refit, isolating a
content-invariant signal from content-specific correlates? For each
model the pipeline is fit on each dataset at the peak-within layer and
evaluated on the other two \emph{without refitting}: a \(3 \times 3\)
matrix, within-dataset CV on the diagonal, pure transfer off it, each
cell scored against the test-dataset majority (layer grids,
frozen-statistics detail, and the TF-IDF transfer control in Appendix
E).

\textbf{Headline.} Averaging margins (probe-acc minus test-majority)
across the 3 within cells and the 6 transfer cells (Table
\ref{tbl:transfer}, Appendix E): Llama retains \(81\%\) of its
within-dataset margin under transfer (\(+18.9\) pp within, \(+15.2\) pp
transferred, \(5/6\) transfers beating the test majority), while
Gemma's retention is \(-54\%\) (the largest within margin of the three,
but two catastrophic collapses with PopQA as target) and Qwen's is
\(-14\%\) (an almost flat probe staying almost flat); per-model detail
in Appendix E.

\textbf{Reading.} Within-dataset peak performance (Llama \(>\) Gemma
\(>\) Qwen) and transfer portability (Llama \(\gg\) Gemma \(\approx\)
Qwen) are \emph{different orderings}: Gemma decodes dataset-specific
content patterns while a Llama direction found on any pool classifies
the others nearly as well as the source. The parsimonious explanation
aligns with §1's finding 2: Llama's heavy calibrated-abstention training
imposes a content-invariant ``the model is uncertain'' representation
the other two trainings did not produce (full \(3 \times 3\) matrices
released; Appendix B.4).
