Faced with a question it cannot answer, a small language model either refuses or confabulates: a fluent, confident, wrong answer. I ask whether that choice is readable from the hidden state before the first generated token, and whether it can be steered. I introduce ConfabQA, a 784-item benchmark whose obscure-pre-cutoff category breaks the correctness/knowledge-cutoff confound, and score every probe against the strongest of four prompt-feature baselines, so only hidden-state information beyond the question text counts. On Qwen3-1.7B, Gemma 2 2B, and Llama 3.2 3B, a late-layer linear direction separates upcoming refusals from confabulations beyond prompt features (+7.4 and +16.3 pp on Qwen and Gemma; +18-25 pp for Llama on external data), and its logit lens is each model's literal refusal-opener vocabulary. Three recoveries of the direction (linear probe, a Qwen-Scope SAE feature, direct optimization) are far from collinear (cosine 0.16-0.64), yet each flips 100% of first tokens to refusal openers at doses at or below the state norm; the single SAE feature 2191 does so at half the probe's dose (30/30 argmaxes), and the reverse push collapses refusals into confabulations while leaving correct answers intact. Mediation is complete at token one: forcing the opener without touching the hidden state yields the same one-in-three judged-refusal rate as any push; the rest revert to content through ordinary autoregressive dynamics. Correctness is model-dependent: Llama's probe adds +21-25 pp over prompt features on PopQA/TriviaQA and keeps 81% of its margin under cross-dataset transfer without refit, while Qwen3-1.7B adds +4-10 pp and Gemma's collapses; a Qwen3-4B control rules out parameter count. No concentrated confabulation feature exists in the dictionary. Benchmark, judge, code, and cached activations are released.
